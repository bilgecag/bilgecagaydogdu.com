Data Scientist

Bilgecag Aydogdu, PhD

Mobile phone data for migration, displacement and official statistics

I am a data scientist who is curious about finding efficient and scalable ways to turn large-scale telecommunications data into population-level statistics. Over the past five years I have built a research portfolio of new methodologies that were tested in various crisis situations. Currently, I work at Positium, where our focus is to put mobile phone data-driven statistics pipelines into production across national statistical offices and government agencies.

About

I was born in Çeşme, Turkey, a town 20 km east of the Greek island of Chios. Perhaps because of the geography of where I grew up, I have been fascinated by borders ever since. How can people be that close, and yet that far?

While I was doing my bachelor's in Istanbul, the Syrian war displaced millions of people. The same border that had fascinated me as a child became a route to safety for hundreds of thousands of them, in a very short time. Yet thousands also lost their lives on those journeys, without ever reaching safety.

This crisis, which started more than a decade ago, is engraved into my professional career. How can we ensure safety for the people who need it most? I look for answers to that question in a rather unconventional way, through big data sources. My work lies at the intersection of many politically sensitive boundaries. In an age of democratic decline, how do we use big data in an ethical and privacy-preserving manner, for the benefit of people?

In my PhD thesis I explored novel ways of measuring and analyzing crisis-induced mobility using mobile phone data sources. More recently, I moved into industry, where I put my efforts into operationalizing these methods so that more people can harness the benefits of this data. I am always up for new connections. If any of my work resonates with you, please get in touch.

Displacement detection

A novel activity space approach to discover displacement patterns

EPJ Data Science, 2025

Rather than tracking single locations, this method models each person's whole activity space and detects when it relocates. Applied to the 2023 Turkiye and Syria earthquakes to identify who was displaced, when, and for how long.

Multi-source measurement

Combining Twitter and mobile phone data to observe border-rush

Journal of Computational Social Science, 2025

When the Turkish-European border opened in 2020, two very different data sources both registered the movement. Cross-validating them shows what each one sees, what each one misses, and how they can be read together.

Novel data sources

Analyzing international airtime top-up transfers for migration and mobility

International Journal of Data Science and Analytics, 2025

When someone abroad tops up a phone back home, that transaction is a trace of a social tie. Across 220 million transfers, those traces sketch migration corridors that conventional statistics struggle to see.
